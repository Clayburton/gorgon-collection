Greco-Roman, 1st–2nd century AD

Athena — Minerva to the Romans — was the goddess of wisdom and cunning, and Odysseus’s constant champion throughout the Odyssey. She pleaded his case before the gods, walked beside him and his son Telemachus in disguise, and guided the wanderer all the long way home. Drawn from Greco-Roman depictions of the first two centuries AD, her helmeted likeness stood as a figure of counsel and protection — the steady hand behind the whole journey.
